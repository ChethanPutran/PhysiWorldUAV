# Sweep fix_v1: repeated baselines, seeds, B3 variants, learning rate and weight decay (pilot v0.6.0, v0.5.0)

Run 2026-11-25. 29 trainings with the fixed trainer (`best.pt` = best validation epoch of the whole run), every run evaluated at that checkpoint on all roles. Tables are generated by `scripts/summarize_sweep.py` from `outputs/eval/fx_*.json` (figures and CSV in `outputs/reports/sweep_fix_v1/`). Position RMSE at 20 steps (0.4 s) [m]. Mean ± standard deviation over seeds where n = 3.

**Limits that apply to every statement here:** pilot data (63 training episodes); the OOD roles have 4 to 7 episodes each; 3 seeds only for the B3 family, B4 and B5b, one seed for B2, B5a, B4 (B3 width) and the learning-rate / weight-decay variants; one dataset for the variant comparison (v0.6.0).

## 1. Results in short

1. **The checkpoint fix is confirmed, and it changes the picture for B4.** At its true best epoch (epoch 1 in all 3 seeds on v0.6.0, also on v0.5.0) B4 is about the physics model: OOD magnitude 0.183 m against B1's 0.185 m. The 0.119 m that the first comparison reported for B4 on OOD magnitude came from a later, worse-on-validation checkpoint (epoch 19) and is not B4's selected result.
2. **None of the B3 variants helps.** B3a (no action in the residual), B3b (zero-residual penalty) and B3c (shared trunk) are within seed noise of base B3 on OOD magnitude (0.167–0.170 against 0.166 m). B3a is worse on test ID (0.040 ± 0.001 against 0.036 ± 0.001 m) and B3a and B3b are worse on OOD physics (0.084 and 0.083 against 0.070 m). B3c equals base B3. So B3's weakness on strong unseen wind is not caused by the action shortcut, the missing zero-residual constraint or the separate decoder/residual heads.
3. **A constant-velocity extrapolation, which learns nothing, is better than every trained model with a physics prior on OOD wind magnitude and direction.** OOD magnitude: constant velocity 0.050 m against B3 0.166, B4 0.183, B1 0.185 m. OOD direction: 0.027 against B3 0.039 and B1 0.058 m. It is also better on OOD trajectory (0.038 against B3 0.047 m). B3 is better on OOD temporal (0.026 against 0.032), OOD gust (0.022 against 0.036) and in distribution (0.036 against 0.072 m). This baseline was missing from the earlier comparison (see §3).
4. **B5b's good OOD-magnitude numbers are the constant-velocity model.** B5b picks its epoch-1 checkpoint (an almost untrained network that predicts about zero acceleration) in 6 of its 7 runs, and its errors equal those of the constant-velocity baseline to three digits (OOD magnitude 0.0504, OOD direction 0.0271). The seed that kept epoch 18 is worse (OOD magnitude 0.132 m). B5b has not learned wind dynamics.
5. **B3 is the best non-oracle model in distribution and on OOD temporal and gust windows, and tied with B4 on test ID.** Test ID: B3 0.036 ± 0.001, B4 0.037 ± 0.001, B1 0.053 m. Validation ID: B3 0.031 against B4 0.037 m (16 %). B3's advantage over B4 on test ID that the first comparison showed (0.035 against 0.050 m) was partly due to B4 being evaluated at a later epoch.
6. **B4 overfits and the two cheap fixes did nothing.** Lower learning rate (2e-4) still selects epoch 2 and ends worse (last-epoch validation 0.0627 m against 0.0505 m). Weight decay 1e-2 selects epoch 1 as the base run does. The two weight-decay runs are effectively no-ops: the decay is applied as lr × wd per step (2e-6 at lr 2e-4), and the validation curves of "lr 2e-4" and "lr 2e-4 + wd 1e-2" differ by at most 2e-5 m. A meaningful weight-decay test needs values around 0.1 to 1.
7. **Checkpoint selection by in-distribution validation hurts out-of-distribution wind.** Validation prefers B4's untrained epoch 1 while a later epoch is better on OOD magnitude (0.119 against 0.183 m). Only a signal that sees OOD wind can select that checkpoint; the OOD validation roles (`val_ood_magnitude`, 3 episodes) exist but are not used for selection.

## 3. A missing baseline: constant velocity

`scripts/constant_velocity_baseline.py` evaluates p_k = p_0 + v_0 · k · Δt with attitude and angular velocity held (zero acceleration), from the true initial state, like B0 and B1; result in `outputs/eval/constant_velocity_v06.json`. It uses no learning and no physics model. Because it beats B1, B3 and B4 on three OOD roles, the earlier statement "B3 is better than physics-only on strong wind" cannot be separated from "windows in these roles have small acceleration". The oracle B6a reaches 0.018 m on OOD magnitude, so the wind force is what the learned models do not recover. Probable reading (not tested): under wind the closed-loop controller's rotor commands partly cancel the wind force, so a model that turns the commands into an acceleration without the wind force (B1, B4, and B3 when its residual is small) predicts an acceleration that does not occur, while zero acceleration is close to the truth.

## 5. Wind-estimator probe on the trained latent (run 2026-11-25, no training)

`evaluation/probe.py`: a frozen ridge regression (fitted on the train role, noise `nominal`) predicts the logged wrench at rollout step k from the model's latent h, from its physical state (v, q, ω and u), or from both. R² is computed per evaluation role on the windows of that role (1.0 = perfect, 0 = as good as the role's mean, negative = worse than the mean). The wrench is used only as the probe's target, never as a model input. `scripts/run_probes.sh`; JSON in `outputs/eval/probe_<run>.json`. Models: v0.6.0 checkpoints at their best validation epoch.

### Positive control: the probe works on a target the latent does carry

Total torque (body frame), R² of h at k = 0, B3 base seed 0: val ID 0.77, test ID 0.82, OOD magnitude 0.82, OOD direction 0.83, OOD gust 0.79 (falling to 0.3–0.4 at k = 20 on the ID roles). So the latent encodes the angular side from the gyro as expected.

### What this shows

- **The latent h of B3 carries no linearly readable wind-force information that transfers across episodes.** This holds for all three base seeds, for B3a, B3b and B3c, and for B2 and B5b, so none of the architectural variants changed it. This is consistent with B3's failure on strong wind (0.166 m, constant-velocity 0.050 m): the residual has nothing wind-related to read from h.
- On the OOD-magnitude role (large wind variance, so R² is meaningful) the physical state alone gives R² = +0.14 and the latent gives −0.15 to −0.27: **the latent is less informative than the state it is built from.**
- Cautions: the probe is linear (a non-linear probe could read more); R² on the ID roles is computed against a small wind variance, so large negative values there partly reflect a small denominator; 63 training episodes; v0.6.0 only; one seed except the base B3.
- Not shown: whether a longer history (audit F3: 0.2 s, latent reset every window) would change this; that needs training.

## 4. What is open

- **Why the residual of B3 does not recover the wind force** in strong wind. Candidates: only 0.2 s of history and a latent reset at every window (audit F3); a training range of wind that does not include the OOD range; the residual's small size (about 4 % of the specific force). The wind-estimator probe (section 5) has since shown that the latent carries no readable wind-force information; which of the candidates is responsible has not been tested.
- **A longer history and wind-range augmentation** are the two training changes that address the candidates above.
- **More seeds for B2, B5a and B4 (B3 width)** and OOD roles with more episodes (Run 6).
- **A stronger weight-decay test** (0.1 to 1) and a selection signal that includes OOD wind, for B4 and B5b.

- **Most of the training data is low wind.** Train: median 1.24 m/s, 19 % of time steps below 0.1 m/s (22 % of the training episodes have no wind: they are the no-wind baselines of the counterfactual groups), 41 % below 1 m/s, 4.3 % above 3 m/s. Per-episode mean wind of the training episodes is 0 to 3.3 m/s.
- **The training maximum is 3.7 m/s.** No ID episode, in any role, exceeds 3.7 m/s peak; there is no episode with a peak between 3.7 and 4.3 m/s.
- **The OOD magnitude role lies completely outside the training range:** 10 episodes (7 test, 3 validation) with peaks 4.3 to 8.2 m/s, median 5.9 m/s (test) and 6.9 m/s (validation), 0 % of time steps below 3 m/s. The other OOD roles (direction, temporal, gust, physics, trajectory) have the same low wind as ID (peaks 1.7 to 3.2 m/s).
- Aerodynamic force grows roughly with speed squared, so at 8 m/s it is about 4.7 times the force at the training maximum (3.7 m/s). The OOD magnitude role is therefore an extrapolation by a factor of 1.6 to 2.2 in speed, not a case between seen conditions. This is a fact about the data; that it explains B3's error on that role (and the probe result of §5) is a hypothesis that wind-range augmentation or wider ID wind (Option C) would test.
