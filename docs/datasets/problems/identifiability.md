# Why Hover-Only Data Cannot Train a Wind-Aware World Model

You've identified the central problem yourself, and it is real. Let me spell out exactly what goes wrong, and then give the fix.

---

## 🔍 The Identifiability Problem

Consider your proposed training objective:

$$
\mathcal{L} = \left\| s_{t+1} - \left[ f_{\text{phys}}(s_t, u_t) + d_\theta(h_t, u_t) \right] \right\|^2
$$

with \(h_t = E_P(a^{\text{IMU}}_t, \omega^{\text{IMU}}_t, u_t)\).

Under hover with your P-D altitude hold **and no horizontal controller**, three things are true:

| Wind (N) | State trajectory | Action trajectory | Residual needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | \(s_0 \to s_1 \to s_2\) (constant) | constant | ~0 |
| 2 | \(s_0 \to s_1 \to s_2\) (constant, **same**) | drifting (drone pushed) | compensates wind |
| 6 | \(s_0 \to s_1 \to s_2\) (constant, **same**) | drifting further | compensates more wind |

If the controller holds position perfectly, **the state trajectory is identical across all wind regimes**. The only difference is the action sequence and the IMU. So:

- \(s_{t+1}\) is the **same** in every training sample given the same \(s_t\).
- The model can learn to output \(s_{t+1} = s_t\) unconditionally and achieve near-zero loss.
- The residual \(d_\theta\) becomes undefined — many residuals fit the same data.

**This is not a bug in your world model; it is a degeneracy in your data.** The model is never forced to identify wind, because wind does not change the trajectory.

---

## ⚠️ Why the IMU Does Not Save You (Fully)

You might argue: "But the IMU does carry the wind signal — accelerometer reads the compensating thrust, gyro shows the attitude correction." That is true, and \(E_P(a_t, \omega_t, u_t)\) can in principle infer \(F_w\). But three problems remain:

### Problem 1 — The residual is trained on state prediction, not wind prediction

\(d_\theta\) is only pushed by \(\|s_{t+1} - \hat s_{t+1}\|^2\). Under hover, that loss is ~0 for the trivial solution. There is no gradient signal forcing \(d_\theta\) to encode wind, because wind has zero effect on the target \(s_{t+1}\).

The model can assign \(d_\theta = 0\) and still fit the data, provided \(f_{\text{phys}}(s_t, u_t)\) produces a state trajectory that matches the observed one. Under wind + compensation, \(f_{\text{phys}}\) will not match (it will predict acceleration), so \(d_\theta\) must correct — but only because the controller's actions happen to make \(f_{\text{phys}}\) wrong. This is a **coincidental** supervision signal, not a designed one.

### Problem 2 — The model can learn "action → residual" instead of "IMU → residual"

Because actions under wind differ systematically, the model can fit:

$$
d_\theta(h_t, u_t) \approx -F_w(u_t)
$$

via the action pathway, ignoring the IMU entirely. This works on the training distribution (wind magnitude correlates with control magnitude) and fails on OOD for exactly the reason you suspect: at test time, the action-wind correlation breaks (e.g., sudden 8 N gust while controller was tuned for 2 N).

### Problem 3 — Hover collapses the transient

The physics of wind is not "force = constant offset." It is "force causes acceleration, which the controller later counters after a delay." Under hover:

- The acceleration phase is ~1 controller time-constant (tens of ms)
- After that, the drone is quasi-static
- The state response to wind is invisible

The model has no data on how wind **evolves the state over time** because the state doesn't evolve. That is exactly what a long-horizon world model needs.

## 🔧 The Correct Data Collection Strategy

You need **four kinds of trajectories**, not one. Each is generated by a distinct controller mode:

### A. Free-flight with random actions (the workhorse)

Turn off position hold. Every \(N\) steps, resample a control action uniformly from \([-1, 1]^4\). Wind is constant. This forces the drone to explore the state-action space under wind:

- The state actually moves — velocity, attitude, position all vary
- \(f_{\text{phys}}(s_t, u_t)\) must predict the physics
- \(d_\theta\) must explain the residual discrepancy = wind
- The model cannot cheat by predicting "stay still"

```python
if step % N == 0:
    action = torch.rand(1, 4, device=device) * 2 - 1
```

### B. Hover with altitude hold (as you have)

Useful for one narrow case, but keep it as **10–20% of the training set**, not 100%. It teaches the model "how to predict near-equilibrium dynamics" — but only if it also has (A).

### C. Step response (transient)

Fly stable hover, then **abruptly change wind** (e.g., step 0 → 5 N at t = 5 s). Log the full ~500 ms transient during which the drone is displaced and the controller counteracts. This is the single most informative sample class for your task — it directly shows wind → state.

### D. Open-loop free fall under wind

No controller. Apply a constant motor command (e.g., 60% thrust) and let the drone fall / drift. The state response is pure wind + physics. This is the strongest physics-supervision signal.

## 📊 A Balanced Data Recipe

For each training trajectory (100–200 s of simulation):

| Segment | Controller | Wind | Duration | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hover (alt hold) | constant | 20 s | Equilibrium dynamics |
| 2 | Free flight, random actions every 0.5 s | constant | 40 s | Explore \(s_{t+1} = F(s_t, u_t, F_w)\) |
| 3 | Hover | step 0→5 N at midpoint | 20 s | Transient response |
| 4 | Hover | sinusoidal | 20 s | Time-varying wind |
| 5 | Open-loop 60% thrust | constant | 20 s | Pure physics under wind |

Then **for the same wind regime**, you also generate OOD-adjacent samples:
- Same trajectory replayed with 2 N → 5 N → 8 N wind (counterfactual pairs!)
- Same trajectory with mass perturbed +10%

The **counterfactual replay** is what lets your world model learn causality rather than correlation. If the same \((s_t, u_t)\) sequence is observed under 2 N and under 8 N wind, and the state transitions differ, the model **must** use the IMU/action to disambiguate — otherwise it cannot fit both.

This is the only way \(d_\theta\) is forced to learn the wind → force mapping.

## 🧠 What the Model Actually Learns (When the Data Is Right)

The training objective, at each step, is:

$$
\hat s_{t+1} = \underbrace{f_{\text{phys}}(s_t, u_t)}_{\text{physics only}} + \underbrace{d_\theta(h_t, u_t)}_{\text{residual}}
$$

Under free-flight with wind:

- \(f_{\text{phys}}\) predicts the rigid-body dynamics assuming no wind (thrust → acceleration, drag → deceleration)
- \(d_\theta\) must account for the **gap** between physics-only and observed
- That gap is precisely \(F_w/m\) projected onto the acceleration
- \(h_t\) is the only thing that can carry wind info at inference time

Under hover-only data:

- \(f_{\text{phys}}\) predicts a motion that never happens
- \(d_\theta\) sees only "compensated" cases — it learns the inverse of the controller, not the wind
- At inference, if the controller is absent or misbehaving, the model has no idea how the drone will actually respond

So the answer to your question — *how does the model learn about forces?* — is: **it learns them from the discrepancy between free-flight observations and physics predictions.** Hover compresses that discrepancy to zero.

# The Three Problems, Explained in Detail

To make the arguments concrete, let me first fix notation.

**Setup:**

- State \(s_t = [\mathbf p_t, \mathbf v_t, \mathbf q_t, \boldsymbol\omega_t] \in \mathbb{R}^{13}\) (position, velocity, quaternion, angular velocity)
- Action \(u_t\) = motor commands or rate targets at step \(t\)
- IMU observation \(a_t, \omega^{\text{IMU}}_t\) (accelerometer + gyro)
- Encoder \(h_t = E_P(a_t, \omega^{\text{IMU}}_t, u_t)\) — the latent from which the decoder pulls information
- Physics predictor \(f_{\text{phys}}(s_t, u_t)\) — an analytic rigid-body model (thrust → acceleration, gravity, no wind)
- Learned residual \(d_\theta(h_t, u_t)\) — a neural network supposed to capture what \(f_{\text{phys}}\) gets wrong (wind, unmodelled drag, mass/inertia errors)
- One-step prediction: \(\hat s_{t+1} = f_{\text{phys}}(s_t, u_t) + d_\theta(h_t, u_t)\)
- Loss: \(\mathcal L = \|s_{t+1} - \hat s_{t+1}\|^2\)

Under wind, the true dynamics are

$$
s_{t+1} = f_{\text{phys}}(s_t, u_t) + g(F_w, s_t, u_t) + \epsilon_t
$$

where \(g\) is the effect of the wind force \(F_w\) (a nonlinear function because of aerodynamic drag and attitude coupling) and \(\epsilon_t\) is simulation noise. The **correct** residual to learn is \(d_\theta^\star \approx g(F_w, s_t, u_t)\).

Each of the three problems is a different way hover-only data prevents \(d_\theta \to d_\theta^\star\).

---

## Problem 1 — The residual is trained on state, not on wind

### What "trained on state" means

The loss is

$$
\mathcal L = \left\| s_{t+1} - f_{\text{phys}}(s_t, u_t) - d_\theta(h_t, u_t) \right\|^2
$$

For the gradient to push \(d_\theta\) toward \(d_\theta^\star\), the following must hold:

$$
\left( s_{t+1} - f_{\text{phys}}(s_t, u_t) \right) \neq 0 \text{ in a way that depends on } F_w
$$

That is, the **observable difference between reality and physics-only must be driven by wind.** If \(s_{t+1} - f_{\text{phys}} \approx 0\) regardless of \(F_w\), then:

- The optimal \(d_\theta\) is the constant function \(d_\theta \equiv 0\)
- The gradient \(\partial \mathcal{L}/\partial d_\theta\) vanishes at \(d_\theta = 0\)
- The network never learns to extract \(F_w\) from \(h_t\) because no loss gradient rewards it

### Why hover makes \(s_{t+1} - f_{\text{phys}} \approx 0\)

Under perfect hover, the controller adjusts the motor commands to cancel wind, so the **observed** acceleration is \(a^{\text{obs}} = 0\). The physics-only prediction, however, assumes no wind and says

$$
f_{\text{phys}}(s_t, u_t) = \frac{T_t}{m}\,\hat z - g\hat z
$$

But \(T_t\) is being set **by the controller in response to wind**. The controller's output \(u_t\) already contains information about the wind — that's the whole point of a compensator. So:

- Wind applies \(F_w\) downward or sideways
- The controller raises \(T\) by roughly the amount needed to cancel it
- The physics model sees "thrust \(T_t\)" and predicts the **correct observed acceleration**
- No residual is needed

**The wind got absorbed into \(u_t\) before \(f_{\text{phys}}\) ever ran.**

### The formal identifiability failure

Consider two hover samples under wind magnitudes \(F_w = 0\) and \(F_w = 4\) N. In both cases the drone hovers; \(s_{t+1}\) is the same. Then:

$$
\begin{cases}
s_{t+1} - f_{\text{phys}}(s_t, u_t^{(0)}) - d_\theta(h_t^{(0)}, u_t^{(0)}) = 0 \\
s_{t+1} - f_{\text{phys}}(s_t, u_t^{(4)}) - d_\theta(h_t^{(4)}, u_t^{(4)}) = 0
\end{cases}
$$

The **actions differ** (\(u_t^{(0)} \neq u_t^{(4)}\)), so \(f_{\text{phys}}\) gives different predictions, so \(d_\theta\) must compensate — **but only via the action argument.** This is the bridge to Problem 2, but for Problem 1 the point is different:

**Wind is not a free variable in the loss.** It is entangled with \(u_t\). There is no sample where the same \(u_t\) is paired with two different \(F_w\). Without such pairs, the model cannot disentangle wind from action — many \((d_\theta, \text{encoder})\) pairs fit the data equally well.

### The "coincidental supervision" wording

The only reason \(d_\theta\) is trained at all is that the controller's \(u_t\) happens to make \(f_{\text{phys}}\) *predict motion when there is none*:

- \(f_{\text{phys}}\) with the boosted \(T_t\) predicts upward acceleration
- Reality is 0 acceleration
- So \(d_\theta\) must push down by \(T_t/m - g\) to cancel
- That correction is \(-F_w\) projected onto acceleration

**But this is not a designed supervision signal.** It depends on the controller being:

1. Compensating (which it is)
2. Logged in the same units the physics model uses
3. Not itself adversarially tuned (e.g., if the controller had a feedforward wind model, \(d_\theta\) would be zero and the wind would be even more invisible)

If you had swapped the controller mid-experiment (say, half trajectories with compensation, half without), the supervision would be inconsistent and \(d_\theta\) would learn a mixture. That is what "coincidental" means: the training signal depends on how the controller happens to interact with \(f_{\text{phys}}\).

### What the fix requires

You need **samples where \(f_{\text{phys}}\) is provably wrong in a way that is linearly attributable to \(F_w\)**. Concretely:

- **Free flight with random actions**: the controller does not compensate, so \(f_{\text{phys}}\) predicts the true rigid-body response minus wind, and the residual is exactly \(F_w/m\)
- **Open-loop trajectories**: motor commands are constant, so the wind effect is unfiltered
- **Step-response pairs**: the same \((s_t, u_t)\) is observed under \(F_w = 0\) and \(F_w \neq 0\) with the same \(u_t\) (achieved by rolling back the state and replaying with different wind)

Any of these restores the identifiability property: \(d_\theta^\star \neq 0\) and \(\partial \mathcal L/\partial d_\theta \neq 0\) at \(d_\theta = 0\).

## Problem 2 — The model can learn action → residual, ignoring IMU

### The alternative model

Suppose the encoder \(E_P\) is a large network with more capacity than necessary. The residual head \(d_\theta\) receives **two inputs**: \(h_t\) (from IMU + action) and \(u_t\) (raw action). In principle it can compute

$$
d_\theta(h_t, u_t) = \phi(u_t)
$$

**ignoring \(h_t\) entirely.** If the training data has a strong statistical correlation

$$
u_t \longleftrightarrow F_w
$$

then the mapping \(u_t \mapsto d_\theta = -F_w(u_t)\) is a valid fit. The model never learns to encode wind from the IMU.

### Why this is not just an implementation flaw — it is a data flaw

Formally, the model class is

$$
\mathcal{M} = \left\{ d_\theta : \mathbb{R}^{\dim h + 4} \to \mathbb{R}^{13} \;\middle|\; \theta \in \Theta \right\}
$$

Inside \(\mathcal{M}\), there are two sub-families:

$$
\mathcal{M}_u = \{ d = \phi(u) : \phi \text{ arbitrary} \}, \qquad \mathcal{M}_{h} = \{ d = \psi(h) : \psi \text{ arbitrary} \}
$$

Both are subsets of \(\mathcal{M}\). On your training data:

$$
\min_{\phi \in \mathcal{M}_u} \mathcal L = \min_{\psi \in \mathcal{M}_h} \mathcal L \approx 0
$$

Because **\(F_w\) is a deterministic function of \(u_t\) under hover-compensation**. The action carries all the wind information statistically, so the IMU carries no *extra* information on the training distribution. Both fits are equally good.

Standard training (stochastic gradient descent with no inductive bias) will find whichever basin it enters first — usually the one that is easier to optimize, which is \(\mathcal{M}_u\) because it is lower-dimensional and has less noise.

### Why it breaks at OOD

At test time, the correlation **action ↔ wind** is broken:

| Test case | \(u_t\) (motor command) | \(F_w\) (wind) | Correlation \(u \leftrightarrow F_w\) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Training | strongly correlated | moderate | ✅ |
| Sudden 8 N gust | controller hasn't adjusted yet — \(u_t\) looks like a 2 N case | 8 N | ❌ |
| Controller failure | \(u_t = 0\) (no input) | 8 N | ❌ |
| Free flight | \(u_t\) arbitrary | moderate | ❌ |

For each of these, the model must predict \(d_\theta = -F_w/m\) from the IMU. But under \(\mathcal{M}_u\), the network has learned "\(u_t \mapsto -F_w(u_t)\)" and cannot generalize because \(u_t\) no longer predicts \(F_w\).

The result: prediction error under OOD balloons, and your world-model's multi-step rollout diverges. The paper "Generalizing to new winds" would fail exactly as the thesis's critic would predict.

### Why "the IMU has the information" is not enough

The IMU **does** contain wind information — the accelerometer reads \(a^{\text{IMU}} = (T + F_w)/m\) in the body frame, and since you also know \(u_t \to T\), you can in principle solve for \(F_w\). So the encoder *could* extract \(F_w\).

But **the loss does not reward it** under the training distribution. There are infinitely many equally-good parameter settings, and only a subset actually use the IMU. Without something to force the model into that subset, it will pick the simpler one.

The three standard remedies:

1. **Break the correlation** in the data (free flight, randomized wind at fixed action, etc.)
2. **Add an auxiliary loss** that directly supervises \(d_\theta\) with an oracle residual — but this contaminates the OOD claim (see your data doc, §1.4: wind is oracle only)
3. **Architectural inductive bias** — e.g., require that \(d_\theta\) be a function of \(h_t\) only, not \(u_t\); or force \(d_\theta(0) = 0\); or apply \(d_\theta\) only via the encoder path

Options 1 and 3 are clean; option 2 violates your evaluation protocol.


# Why Architectural Inductive Bias Helps

Short answer: **an architectural constraint does not add information — it removes a shortcut.** The information about wind is already in the data (in the IMU stream). But the model has no incentive to use it because the action stream provides an easier route to the same loss reduction. Forcing the residual to depend only on the encoder is a way of **deleting the easy route**, so the optimizer is left with only the intended one.

Let me explain this precisely.

---

## The General Principle

The hypothesis space is

$$
\mathcal{H} = \left\{ d_\theta : (h_t, u_t) \mapsto \hat{s}_{t+1} - f_{\text{phys}}(s_t, u_t) \right\}
$$

This space is **very large**. Two subsets of it fit the training data equally well:

- **\(\mathcal{H}_u\)**: functions of \(u_t\) only — \(d_\theta = \phi(u_t)\)
- **\(\mathcal{H}_h\)**: functions of \(h_t\) only — \(d_\theta = \psi(h_t)\)

Under hover-compensation, both achieve near-zero loss because \(u_t\) and \(F_w\) are in one-to-one correspondence:

$$
u_t^{\text{cmd}} \;\leftrightarrow\; F_w
$$

**SGD does not find the "right" answer; it finds the easiest one.** Given a 4-dim input \(u_t\) and a 256-dim input \(h_t\), the function \(\phi(u_t)\) has:

- Fewer parameters (4 → 13 outputs, vs. 256 → 13)
- Shorter computation graph (fewer layers before reaching the output)
- Lower gradient variance (small input noise propagates less)

So SGD **prefers** \(\mathcal{H}_u\). The model learns the shortcut.

**Inductive bias** = manually restricting \(\mathcal H\) so the shortcut is not a valid hypothesis. If you force \(d_\theta\) to be a function of \(h_t\) only:

$$
\mathcal H' = \mathcal H_h \subset \mathcal H, \qquad \mathcal H_u \not\subset \mathcal H'
$$

Now \(\phi(u_t)\) is not expressible, and the optimizer must use \(\psi(h_t)\). You cannot choose the wrong solution if it is not in the space.

---

## Why Each of the Three Suggestions Helps

### Option A — Make \(d_\theta\) a function of \(h_t\) only, not \(u_t\)

Removes the direct action path. The model must now route action information **through** the encoder:

$$
u_t \;\longrightarrow\; h_t \;\longrightarrow\; d_\theta
$$

That extra step matters because:

1. The encoder is **jointly trained with the state decoder** \(D_s\), which must reconstruct \(s_t\) from \(h_t\). Under multi-task pressure, the encoder is incentivized to encode state-relevant information, not just raw \(u_t\). The 256-dim latent is *shared*, so preserving \(u_t\) costs capacity that could go toward reconstructing \(s_t\).

2. The latent is a **bottleneck** — if it is small (say 32-dim), the encoder cannot preserve all of \(u_t\) without losing the ability to reconstruct \(s_t\). So the encoder learns a *compressed* representation that contains what's necessary for downstream losses, not the trivial passthrough.

3. The loss backpropagates through \(E_P\), not just through \(d_\theta\). So the encoder receives gradients specifically shaped to produce useful wind information. It learns an internal representation that is causally aligned, not statistically aligned.

**Caveat**: option A alone is weak if the latent is large. Under hover-compensation data, \(u_t \in \mathbb{R}^4\) fits trivially into \(h_t \in \mathbb{R}^{256}\). The model can just copy \(u_t\) into a 4-dim subspace of \(h_t\). To close this, the ideal is:

- **Make the residual head use only IMU-derived features**: \(d_\theta(E_{\text{IMU}}(a_t, \omega^{IMU}_t))\), with a separate IMU encoder that does not see \(u_t\)
- Or add an **auxiliary loss** that penalizes action predictability from \(h_t\): \(\| \hat{u}_t - u_t \|^2\) with \(\hat{u}_t = g(h_t)\) minimized to have low capacity — the encoder then avoids encoding action trivially

### Option B — Force \(d_\theta(0) = 0\)

This is a **regularizer against constant bias**. Without it, the model can learn

$$
d_\theta(h_t, u_t) = c \quad \text{(constant)}
$$

which is optimal under hover-compensation (the residual is a constant wind force projection). A constant function trivially achieves low training loss and generalizes poorly — it cannot adapt to changes in wind or action.

Forcing \(d_\theta(0) = 0\) means: *the residual is only nonzero when the latent is nonzero*. It restricts the solution to functions that are "caused" by the input. Under sparse latents (e.g., L1-regularized), this biases the model toward putting "wind" in a few active dimensions.

**Caveat**: \(d_\theta(0) = 0\) does not eliminate \(\mathcal{H}_u\) — it just adds a bias. If the encoder stores \(u_t\) in \(h_t\) and \(d_\theta\) learns \(\phi(u_t)\) from that stored copy, the constraint is satisfied but the shortcut is preserved. **It must be combined with Option A or A-prime.**

### Option C — Apply \(d_\theta\) only via the encoder path

Instead of computing \(d_\theta\) as a separate head that receives \(h_t\) and \(u_t\), **integrate it into the decoder**:

$$
\hat{s}_{t+1} = f_{\text{phys}}(s_t, u_t) + D_s(h_t) - D_s^{\text{phys}}(s_t, u_t)
$$

where \(D_s\) is the state decoder and \(D_s^{\text{phys}}\) is the physics-only prediction. In this form, the residual is a natural byproduct of the decoder, not an auxiliary head. The decoder has a **single, shared computation** — the model cannot "turn off" the encoder for the residual path without harming its ability to reconstruct \(s_t\).

**Why this matters**: separate heads have separate parameters. The residual head can learn to ignore \(h_t\) entirely, and the encoder and state decoder can learn their own thing without interference. Integrating the paths forces **representation sharing** and cannot drift.

**Caveat**: this is the weakest of the three in isolation. If \(D_s\) is large enough, it can still encode \(\phi(u_t)\) inside its own computation graph — nothing prevents it. It only helps when combined with explicit constraints (e.g., that the encoder is a bottleneck, or that \(D_s\) has limited capacity).

---

## The Crucial Caveat: Architecture Alone Is Not Enough

**Architectural bias only works if the intended pathway contains the required information.**

Ask: **does the IMU under hover-compensation carry enough information to reconstruct \(F_w\)?**

The accelerometer reads (body frame):

$$
a_{\text{IMU}} = R^\top\left( \frac{T(u_t)}{m}\hat z_{\text{body}} - g\hat z_{\text{world}} \right) + R^\top \frac{F_w}{m}
$$

Under perfect hover, \(F_w\) is *fully compensated* by the controller via attitude (\(R \neq I\), tilted into the wind) and thrust. The IMU reading has the wind contribution **mixed into** the specific force — and to extract it, you need to know:

1. The current attitude \(R\) (from the gyro-integrated quaternion, or state estimator)
2. The thrust \(T(u_t)\) — which requires knowing \(u_t\)

**So under hover, the IMU does NOT trivially encode wind — it encodes a superposition that requires action and attitude to decode.** If you remove \(u_t\) entirely, the encoder cannot disentangle thrust from wind — the residual head sees a correlated bundle and has to learn an implicit decomposition from data.

Under **free flight** (no compensation), the situation is much cleaner:

$$
a_{\text{IMU}} = R^\top\left( \frac{T(u_t)}{m}\hat z_{\text{body}} \right) + R^\top\frac{F_w}{m}
$$

Now \(F_w/m\) produces a *distinct, uncancelled* acceleration that shows up directly in the IMU reading and in the observable state trajectory. This is what the residual can learn to extract.

**Therefore:**

| Data type | IMU carries wind? | Architecture alone sufficient? |
|---|---|---|
| Hover-compensation | Indirectly (needs action to decode) | ❌ No |
| Free flight (random actions) | Directly | ✅ Yes (with Option A) |
| Step response | Directly, with transients | ✅ Yes |
| Open-loop | Directly | ✅ Yes |

---

## The Correct Combination

The architectural bias is **necessary but not sufficient**. The order of importance:

### 1. Fix the data first (highest impact)

Generate free-flight trajectories with random actions, step-response trajectories, and counterfactual replays. This gives the IMU direct wind information and breaks the \(u_t \leftrightarrow F_w\) correlation. Without this, no architecture can learn wind.

### 2. Apply architectural bias (amplifies the data fix)

- **Use \(d_\theta\) from an IMU-only encoder**: \(d_\theta(E_{\text{IMU}}(a_t, \omega^{IMU}_t))\) — this literally forbids the action shortcut
- **Add \(d_\theta(0) = 0\)** via a soft penalty \(\lambda \| d_\theta(0) \|^2\) in the loss, not a hard architectural constraint (so the model isn't brittle to encoder mean shifts)
- **Apply the residual via a shared decoder path** to force representation sharing

### 3. Add an auxiliary regularizer (optional, only for diagnosis)

Train a small "wind estimator" from the latent: \(\hat{F}_w = h_\phi(h_t)\). If it can predict \(F_w\) accurately, the latent carries wind info (the encoder is doing its job). If not, the model has learned wind via some other pathway and the OOD claim is compromised. This is a **diagnostic**, not a training objective, so it does not violate your data requirements document (§1.4: wind is oracle only).

---

## Summary

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Why does architecture help at all?** | It restricts the hypothesis space. By removing the easy route \(\phi(u_t)\), SGD is forced to find \(\psi(h_t)\). |
| **Why \(d_\theta(h_t)\) only?** | Removes the direct action shortcut. Forces routing through the encoder, which is jointly trained with state reconstruction and therefore learns to encode *causal* features. |
| **Why \(d_\theta(0) = 0\)?** | Regularizes against constant bias. Prevents the model from learning a trivial constant residual. Weak alone; useful in combination. |
| **Why apply via the encoder path?** | Forces weight sharing. The encoder cannot "do its own thing" for the state decoder while ignoring the residual; both must share the same computation. |
| **Is architecture alone enough?** | **No.** Architecture works only if the intended pathway (IMU) carries wind information. Under hover-compensation, the IMU carries wind only through a superposition that requires action to decode, so the IMU does not trivially reveal wind. |
| **What is required?** | (1) Free-flight/step-response data so the IMU sees wind directly; (2) \(d_\theta\) from IMU-only; (3) shared decoder path. Together, these make the OOD claim defensible. |

Your instinct that "the model can cheat by reading action" is exactly the identifiability failure. The architectural bias is the fix for **that** failure — but not for the data-degeneracy failure, which requires changing the trajectories you collect.


# Dataset Recipe for World-Model Training Across Flight Modes

Before the per-mode tables, three design principles govern every row. Every mode must satisfy all three, or the residual \(d_\theta\) becomes unlearnable (Problems 1–3 from earlier).

| Principle | What it means for a mode |
|---|---|
| **Identifiability** | \(s_{t+1} - f_{\text{phys}}(s_t, u_t)\) must depend on \(F_w\) and be nonzero for some samples. This fails under perfect hover-compensation (Problem 1). |
| **Decorrelation** | \(u_t\) must **not** be a deterministic function of \(F_w\). This fails under all hover-compensation (Problem 2). |
| **Full transient** | The state must evolve during the wind disturbance. This fails under hover (Problem 3). |

**Every mode below is designed to violate at least one of the failure conditions in a controlled way.**

---

## Mode 1 — Hover (Altitude Hold)

### What it teaches
Equilibrium dynamics: the drone can predict "state stays put" when the controller is active, and can account for the compensating thrust. This is the **only mode** where wind is fully absorbed into \(u_t\), so it is the *weakest* supervision. It is included for completeness but must be the smallest segment.

| Dimension | Specification |
|---|---|
| **State variation** | \(\mathbf p \in [\pm 0.1]\) m jitter, \(\mathbf v \approx 0\) (\(|\mathbf v| < 0.05\) m/s), \(\mathbf q \approx \text{identity}\) (tilt \(< 5°\)), \(\boldsymbol\omega \approx 0\) |
| **Action exploration** | Narrow: thrust near hover (\(T \in [0.95\,mg, 1.05\,mg]\)), roll/pitch/yaw rates \(\in [-0.05, 0.05]\) |
| **Altitude** | **Four fixed altitudes**, not one: 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.5 m. De-correlates altitude from state, so the model does not overfit to \(z = 1.5\) |
| **Wind** | Constant at 0, 1, 2, 4 N (four sub-runs). Also a "step 0 → X N" sub-run for the compensating transient |
| **Transient content** | ~0 (that is the point of this mode). Do **not** use hover for OOD validation |
| **Counterfactual pairs** | Optional — hover is degenerate for causal pairing anyway |

**Trajectory structure (per trajectory):**
```
0–5s   wind = 0 N        (establish hover)
5–10s  wind = constant   (compensating steady state)
10–12s wind stepped to +2 N higher  (transient — 2s only)
12–15s wind constant (new level)    (settle)
```
Total 15 s per hover trajectory. Altitude is picked from {0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.5} uniformly.

**Data volume:** 10% of total (≈10 trajectories).

**Why 10% and not 0%:** the model needs *some* equilibrium data so that during OOD evaluation, hover predictions do not explode. But if you use 100% hover, the model learns the trivial "\(s_{t+1} = s_t\)" answer (Problem 1).

---

## Mode 2 — Translational Motion

### What it teaches
The workhorse. Wind under translational flight interacts with the state via **aerodynamic drag** — the effective wind force is roughly \(F_{\text{drag}} \propto (\mathbf v - \mathbf v_{\text{wind}})^2\), so the same wind produces different residuals at different speeds. This is the cleanest identifiability signal for \(d_\theta\).

| Dimension | Specification |
|---|---|
| **State variation** | \(\mathbf v\): 0.2–2.0 m/s, all three axes exercised separately then combined. \(\mathbf p\): ±5 m displacement from origin. Tilt: 5–25° (differential thrust provides acceleration, tilt follows). \(\boldsymbol\omega\): small (< 0.5 rad/s) except during direction changes |
| **Action exploration** | **Two sub-types per trajectory:**<br>(a) *Scripted*: straight-line, circle (r = 2 m), figure-eight (40% of time)<br>(b) *Random*: sample \(u_t\) uniformly from \([-0.6, 0.6]^4\) every 0.3 s (60% of time) |
| **Altitude** | Slow altitude ramps 1.0 → 2.0 m over the trajectory, so \(z\) is not fixed |
| **Wind** | Three wind orientations **per trajectory**:<br>- **Crosswind** (perpendicular to motion): drift + roll coupling<br>- **Headwind** (against motion): increased drag, deceleration<br>- **Tailwind** (with motion): reduced effective drag, acceleration |
| **Transient content** | Constant-speed flight gives steady state; direction changes give 200–400 ms transients; random action segments give continuous transients |
| **Counterfactual pairs** | **Required.** For each scripted segment, replay with 0 N and 2× the nominal wind |

**Trajectory structure:**
```
0–10s    scripted: straight line +X, crosswind
10–20s   scripted: straight line +Y, crosswind
20–30s   scripted: circle r=2m, wind from +X
30–40s   scripted: figure-eight, wind stepped at t=35s
40–70s   random actions, constant 2 N wind
70–100s  random actions, wind stepped every 10s among {0, 2, 4, 6} N
```

**Data volume:** 25–30% of total (≈30 trajectories × 100 s).

**Why this is the workhorse:** the residual \(s_{t+1} - f_{\text{phys}}(s_t, u_t)\) becomes a *strong, v-dependent function of \(F_w\)*. Random actions break the action-wind correlation. Both Problems 1 and 2 are defeated here.

---

## Mode 3 — Acceleration and Deceleration

### What it teaches
The **direct signal** \(a = (T + F_w)/m - g\hat z\). Under free acceleration, \(f_{\text{phys}}\) predicts the rigid-body acceleration correctly, but with the wrong \(T\) (because the controller has not yet compensated for the wind). The residual is **exactly** the wind-induced acceleration \(F_w/m\), and its sign flips with thrust direction.

| Dimension | Specification |
|---|---|
| **State variation** | \(a_t\): \(\pm 3\) m/s² in z, \(\pm 2\) m/s² in x/y. \(\mathbf v\): cross-couples with \(a_t\) during ramps. Tilt: 20–40° during aggressive accel (thrust vectoring) |
| **Action exploration** | **Ramp profiles:**<br>- Thrust ramp: \(T\) from 0.6 → 1.4 \(\times mg\) over 3 s, hold 2 s, back down<br>- Thrust step: \(T\) jumps by ±40% \(mg\) in 1 step<br>- Thrust sinusoid: 0.2 Hz, amplitude 30% of hover thrust<br>- Random rate commands + thrust ramp |
| **Altitude** | 1.5–3.0 m — need headroom so ramps do not hit ground |
| **Wind** | Constant at **two magnitudes per trajectory** (0 and 4 N) so ramp direction × wind direction is exercised. Additionally, a step mid-ramp to test transient during acceleration |
| **Transient content** | **Primary.** Ramp duration × \(1/dt\) = 300 samples of clean acceleration transient. This is where the residual carries the most information |
| **Counterfactual pairs** | **Required.** Same ramp, \(F_w = 0\) vs \(F_w = 4\) N. The residual difference is exactly \(F_w/m\) |

**Trajectory structure:**
```
0–15s    thrust ramps in z, wind=0
15–30s   thrust ramps in z, wind=4 N
30–45s   thrust ramps in z, wind=step 0→4 N at t=37s  (transient during ramp!)
45–60s   thrust ramps in x (forward accel), wind=0
60–75s   thrust ramps in x (forward accel), wind=4 N headwind
75–90s   combined ramp: T and roll command simultaneously, wind=2 N
90–100s  thrust sinusoid + wind step
```

**Data volume:** 15–20% of total (≈20 trajectories × 100 s).

**Why this is essential:** acceleration is the *only* mode where \(f_{\text{phys}}\) is provably wrong under wind. Under hover, \(f_{\text{phys}}\) matches the compensated thrust. Under acceleration, \(f_{\text{phys}}\) uses raw \(T\) while the true force is \(T + F_w\). The residual must therefore equal \(F_w/m\), giving the model an unambiguous target.

---

## Mode 4 — Yaw Motion

### What it teaches
Wind direction is defined in the **world frame**, but the drone's aerodynamics are in the **body frame**. As the drone yaws, the effective wind angle changes — even when \(F_w\) is constant. This forces the model to learn the frame transformation \(\mathbf R^\top F_w\). Without yaw data, the model can succeed on any single flight direction but will fail when the drone turns.

| Dimension | Specification |
|---|---|
| **State variation** | \(\psi\) (yaw angle): full \(-\pi\) to \(+\pi\) coverage. \(\omega_z\): \(\pm 0.5\) to \(\pm 1.5\) rad/s during maneuvers, \(\approx 0\) otherwise. \(\mathbf p, \mathbf v\): small (hover-like) for yaw-only segments; nonzero for yaw-while-translating |
| **Action exploration** | **Three sub-modes:**<br>(a) *Yaw-only*: \(\omega_z = \pm 0.5\) rad/s constant for 5 s, then ±1.0, then ±1.5<br>(b) *Yaw sweep*: \(\omega_z = 0.5 \sin(2\pi \cdot 0.2 \cdot t)\) (0.2 Hz sinusoid)<br>(c) *Yaw while translating*: circle with yaw tangent to the circle |
| **Altitude** | 1.0–2.0 m constant (isolate yaw from altitude dynamics) |
| **Wind** | Constant wind at **four directions** relative to body: +X, +Y, −X, −Y. This means 4 sub-runs per yaw trajectory, each with a different wind direction. Critical: the same \(\omega_z\) must be paired with different world-frame \(F_w\) |
| **Transient content** | Yaw rate steps (~200 ms transient), yaw-stops (overshoot), attitude coupling during aggressive yaw |
| **Counterfactual pairs** | **Required.** Same yaw sequence, wind direction rotated 90° between runs |

**Trajectory structure:**
```
0–10s    hover, wind +X
10–20s   yaw sinusoid 0.2 Hz, wind +X
20–30s   yaw step-and-hold at π/2, wind +X
30–40s   yaw step-and-hold at π, wind +X
40–50s   yaw sinusoid, wind +Y   ← same action, wind rotated
50–60s   yaw step-and-hold, wind +Y
60–70s   circle with yaw tangent, wind +X
70–80s   circle with yaw tangent, wind +Y
80–100s  random: yaw + small translation, wind step at t=90s
```

**Data volume:** 10–15% of total (≈12 trajectories × 100 s).

**Why 10–15% and not more:** yaw is one dimension and the mode is narrow. But without it, the world-model cannot generalize across OOD wind directions — and directional OOD is one of your thesis's explicit regimes.

---

## Mode 5 — Moderate Attitude Changes

### What it teaches
Attitude is where **wind torque** matters. Wind that is not aligned with the drone's symmetry axis creates a torque \(\tau_w = r \times F_w\) at the center of pressure, which is offset from the center of gravity. This is a state dimension (quaternion + angular velocity) that has a *different* wind sensitivity than position. Without this mode, the model has no data on how wind rotates the drone.

| Dimension | Specification |
|---|---|
| **State variation** | Roll \(\phi\): \(-30°\) to \(+30°\). Pitch \(\theta\): \(-30°\) to \(+30°\). Yaw \(\psi\): small. Quaternion components span the full \([-1, 1]\). \(\boldsymbol\omega\): \(\pm 0.5\) to \(\pm 1.0\) rad/s during attitude maneuvers |
| **Action exploration** | **Three sub-modes:**<br>(a) *Attitude hold steps*: command roll = +15°, hold, then +30°, hold, then back to 0<br>(b) *Attitude sine*: \(\phi = 20° \sin(2\pi \cdot 0.3 \cdot t)\), separate run for pitch, separate run for combined roll-pitch<br>(c) *Random attitude commands*: sample roll/pitch rates from \([-0.5, 0.5]\) rad/s, hold for 0.5 s, resample |
| **Altitude** | 1.0–2.0 m (attitude maneuvers do not need altitude variation; keep it constant within each sub-mode) |
| **Wind** | **Vertical wind is critical.** Run each sub-mode with:<br>- Horizontal wind (baseline)<br>- Vertical wind (+\(z\))<br>- Vertical wind (−\(z\)) — simulates updraft/downdraft<br>- Diagonal (combined) |
| **Transient content** | Attitude steps give ~300–500 ms transients. Sine gives continuous oscillation. Random steps give irregular transients |
| **Counterfactual pairs** | **Required.** Same roll/pitch commands, wind switched between horizontal and vertical |

**Trajectory structure:**
```
0–15s    attitude step sequence (roll, then pitch), wind horizontal
15–30s   attitude step sequence, wind vertical +Z
30–45s   attitude sine (roll), wind horizontal
45–60s   attitude sine (roll), wind vertical +Z  ← isolates the vertical wind effect
60–75s   combined roll-pitch sine, wind horizontal
75–90s   combined roll-pitch sine, wind diagonal
90–100s  random attitude + wind step at t=95s
```

**Data volume:** 15% of total (≈15 trajectories × 100 s).

**Why 15%:** attitude has 7 of the 13 state dimensions (\(\mathbf q\) + \(\boldsymbol\omega\)). If you under-sample this mode, the model cannot predict \(s_{t+1}\)'s attitude components under OOD wind — which is exactly where the quaternion distance metric \(d_{SO(3)}\) in your proposal will fail first.

## Mode 6 — Combined Translational and Rotational

### What it teaches
The **coupled manifold**. In real flight, translation and rotation are never isolated. The world model must handle the nonlinear interaction: translational velocity affects wind drag, which creates torque, which tilts the drone, which changes the thrust vector, which changes acceleration. This is where the model generalizes (or fails to).

| Dimension | Specification |
|---|---|
| **State variation** | **All 13 dimensions vary.** \(\mathbf v\): 0.5–2 m/s. Tilt: 15–40°. \(\boldsymbol\omega\): 0.3–1.5 rad/s. \(z\): ramps through 1–3 m |
| **Action exploration** | **Waypoint-based + aggressive maneuvers:**<br>- Figure-eight in xy-plane with yaw tracking the tangent (30%)<br>- Helix: circle in xy while climbing in z, yaw tangent (30%)<br>- Random waypoints every 2 s, sample from \(\pm 3\) m sphere (40%) |
| **Altitude** | 1.0–3.0 m — full range exercised in helix |
| **Wind** | **All wind types represented:**<br>- Constant (4 magnitudes)<br>- Step (0→4 N and 4→0 N)<br>- Sinusoidal (0.1 Hz, 0.3 Hz)<br>- Gust (2 N → 6 N for 0.5 s → back)<br>- Dryden turbulence (\(\sigma = 0.5, 1.5, 2.5\)) |
| **Transient content** | Continuous — waypoints change every 2 s, and wind is time-varying, so the state is always in motion |
| **Counterfactual pairs** | **Strongly required.** Replay each trajectory under 3× wind magnitude and 0 wind |

**Trajectory structure (100 s single trajectory):**
```
0–20s    figure-eight, wind=2N constant
20–40s   figure-eight, wind=step 2→6N at t=30s
40–55s   helix climb, wind=sinusoidal 0.2Hz amp 3N
55–70s   helix descent, wind=gust at t=62s
70–85s   random waypoints, wind=dryden σ=1.5
85–100s  random waypoints, wind=step 6→0 at t=92s
```

**Data volume:** 15–20% of total (≈18 trajectories × 100 s).

**Why 15–20%:** this is the mode that most resembles evaluation conditions. Under-trained, the model overfits to isolation modes and fails on the OOD test set — which is exactly the failure mode a reviewer will flag.

## The Counterfactual Pair Requirement

This is the single most important addition. **For every scripted segment, replay the same action sequence with different wind.** Specifically:

```
Trajectory A:  scripted actions, wind = 0 N
Trajectory B:  same actions, wind = 2 N
Trajectory C:  same actions, wind = 4 N (or OOD level)
```

This gives you triples \((s_t, u_t, F_{w}^{(1)}), (s_t, u_t, F_w^{(2)}), (s_t, u_t, F_w^{(3)})\) that share \(s_t\) and \(u_t\) but have different wind. **The only way the model can fit all three is to use the IMU to disambiguate.** This is the direct countermeasure to Problem 2.

The counterfactual pairs are stored as separate trajectories with a `pair_id` field in the metadata, so the training loop can enforce them.

## Sanity Checks Before Training

For every mode, before you commit the data to the world model:

| Check | Method | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| **State coverage** | Compute per-dimension std dev of \(s\) across all trajectories | Each of 13 state dims has std > 0 (no dimension is frozen) |
| **Residual magnitude** | Compute \(\bar{\|\Delta_t\|}\) where \(\Delta_t = s_{t+1} - f_{\text{phys}}(s_t, u_t)\) | Should grow linearly with \(F_w\) magnitude (not constant, not zero) |
| **Decorrelation** | Correlation \(\rho(u_t, F_w)\) across samples | \(\rho \in [-0.3, 0.3]\) (weak correlation only) |
| **Transient content** | Mean \(\|\dot{s}_t\|\) per mode | Nonzero in every mode (even hover should have a small \(\dot{s}\)) |
| **Counterfactual pairing** | For each `pair_id`, verify same \((s_t, u_t)\) sequence | Present in at least 20% of scripted segments |

If the **residual magnitude check** fails (residual constant or zero), then \(d_\theta\) is not learnable under that mode — go back and check whether the mode is being flown with compensation (Problem 1) or without action variation (Problem 2).

## The One-Sentence Summary

**Every mode must either force \(f_{\text{phys}}\) to be wrong in a way attributable to wind (free acceleration, free translation), or break the action-wind correlation (random actions, counterfactual pairs).** Hover is the only mode where wind is fully absorbed into \(u_t\), which is why it is 10% of the data, not 100%. If you generate the six modes at the volumes above, \(d_\theta\) becomes identifiable, decorrelated, and transient-rich — which is exactly what the world-model needs to generalize to OOD wind.
