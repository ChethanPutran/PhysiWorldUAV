# Rotor-Drag Envelope at OOD Wind — Analysis and Recommendation

## 1. What the envelope constraint actually means

The linear rotor-drag model in `forces/rotor_drag.py` assumes the rotor's **advance ratio**

$$\mu \;=\; \frac{V_\infty}{\Omega R}$$

(where $V_\infty$ is the free-stream velocity, $\Omega$ the rotor speed, and $R$ the blade radius) is **small enough that the drag–velocity relationship is linear**. Blade-element momentum theory produces a linear term $F_d \propto \mu$ plus a quadratic correction $F_d \propto \mu^2$. The linear model is within ~5–15% of the full non-linear model **up to** $\mu \approx 0.15$, degrades to ~15–20% by $\mu = 0.2$, and rapidly gets worse after.

Your threshold of 6.8 m/s corresponds to:

$$\mu = 0.15 \;\Rightarrow\; V_\infty = 0.15 \cdot \Omega_\text{hover} R$$

With `radius_rotor = 0.08 m` and $\Omega_\text{hover} \approx 453$ rad/s, that gives $\Omega R \approx 36.2$ m/s → $V \approx 5.4$ m/s. That doesn't match your 6.8 m/s, so your hover rotor speed or radius must differ — but the physics is the same. **The linear model runs out at 6.8 m/s because your `Ω·R` (tip speed) makes it so.**

---

## 2. The trade-off in plain terms

| Choice | What you preserve | What you give up |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** μ ≤ 0.2, keep OOD in (4, 8] | The thesis's OOD test — the model is actually tested on high wind, as claimed | ~15–20% error in the "ground-truth" drag at 7.4–8.2 m/s; must state this limitation |
| **(b)** μ ≤ 0.15, cap OOD at ~6.5 m/s | Ground truth is physically defensible everywhere | The OOD test no longer tests high wind — the (4, 6.5] range is a **1.5 m/s mild extension** of ID, not real OOD |

The question is not "which is more accurate" — (b) is. The question is: **which failure mode does the thesis care more about?**

- (b)'s failure mode: **the OOD claim becomes trivial.** The model trained on [0, 4] m/s and evaluated on (4, 6.5] m/s never sees a wind regime it can't already handle. The reported "OOD degradation" is a rounding error, not a meaningful test.
- (a)'s failure mode: **the ground truth is wrong by a bounded amount at high wind.** The error is deterministic, structured, and learnable — but it's not "true physical wind."

For a thesis whose central contribution is *OOD generalization under hidden wind*, **the test must actually be OOD**. Option (b) makes the thesis's central experiment null.

---

## 3. Why (a) is the right call — the deeper argument

The key insight is that **research OOD is about physical stress, not physical perfection**. What makes the OOD experiment meaningful is *not* that the 8 m/s data perfectly captures real-world rotor aerodynamics. It's that:

1. **The 8 m/s data is *harder* than the 4 m/s data** — different aerodynamic regime, larger disturbance, more nonlinear response.
2. **The ground truth is *internally consistent*** — the same linear model produces both the ID and OOD data, so the model's performance gap between ID and OOD is measured against the *same* physics.
3. **The error is *learnable*** — a model that sees the 8 m/s data doesn't need to know that the drag is "really" non-linear; it just needs to learn the dynamics the simulator produces. That dynamics is a well-defined function of state and action, and the model's job is to learn *that function*, not "real physics."

Under these three conditions, the OOD test **is valid** — it measures "can the model generalize from low-μ to high-μ dynamics?" — which is exactly what the thesis wants to answer. It just isn't measuring "can the model learn real-world high-wind aerodynamics?" That's a *different* (and harder) question the thesis doesn't claim to answer.

**The limitation you must state** (in the thesis, the README, and the dataset metadata):

> "For OOD episodes with mean wind > 6.8 m/s, the linear rotor-drag approximation is stretched to $\mu \in [0.15, 0.2]$. The linear model underestimates the non-linear rotor drag by up to ~20% in this range. This does not affect the model's ability to learn the dynamics the simulator produces — only its ability to predict real-world flight in this regime. All OOD results are therefore reported as *relative degradation under increased aerodynamic load*, not absolute accuracy against real flight."

That framing is honest, testable, and preserves the experimental design. It's a *stronger* claim than (b)'s "we tested where the model was already valid."

## 4. The hybrid refinement — (a) plus filtering

The best of both worlds is (a) **plus per-episode metadata** so a reader can filter:

**In `EpisodeMetadata.wind_parameters`, add:**

```yaml
rotor_drag_envelope_ratio: 0.18   # = max_t |u(t)| / (Omega * R)
rotor_drag_envelope_status: "marginal"   # "valid" | "marginal" | "exceeded"
```

Then in `analysis/diagnostics.py` and `evaluation/metrics.py`, report each OOD metric twice:

- **Full OOD set**: all episodes with mean wind > 4 m/s, including marginal.
- **Strict OOD set**: episodes with $\mu \le 0.15$ only (excluding the marginal ones).

If both give the same qualitative ranking (residual beats physics-only beats pure-neural), the thesis claim is robust to the modelling error. If they disagree, the reader knows to weigh the marginal episodes carefully.

This costs almost nothing to implement (one metadata field, one filter in analysis), and it transforms the limitation from "we glossed over it" into "we tested the robustness of our conclusions against it."

## 5. Critical check before implementing (a)

**Verify `RotorDragConfig.radius_rotor` matches the actual Hummingbird blade.**

The Hummingbird research platform (GRASP Lab UPenn, also in OmniDrones' assets) uses **~13 cm (0.13 m) propellers**, not 0.08 m. If your `RotorDragConfig` still has `radius_rotor = 0.08` (which appears to be the case in the config we've been working with), then:

$$V(\mu = 0.15) = 0.15 \cdot \Omega_\text{hover} \cdot 0.13 \approx 8.8 \text{ m/s}$$

**With the correct blade radius, the linear model is already valid throughout your entire OOD range (4, 8] m/s. The envelope problem disappears entirely.**

This must be checked before choosing between (a) and (b). If the radius is genuinely 0.08 m for your specific build, then (a) is the right call. If it's actually 0.13 m and 0.08 was a placeholder, then **the correct fix is to update the config**, and neither (a) nor (b) is needed.

To verify: check the Hummingbird USD asset (usually `OmniDrones/omni_drones/robots/assets/usd/hummingbird/hummingbird.usd`), the `hummingbird.yaml` in the same directory, and the `radius_rotor` field in your `forces.yaml`. The three must agree.

| Question | Answer |
